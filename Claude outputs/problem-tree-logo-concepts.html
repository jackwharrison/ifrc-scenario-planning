Problem Tree Builder — logo concepts

Six directions, each shown large, on dark, and at favicon sizes (32px and 18px). Colours use a deep red and slate grey so they sit comfortably next to IFRC branding without copying it.

32px · 18px

1. Node tree

A trunk splitting into branches that end in data points: causes at the roots, effects at the top. Reads as both a tree and a network diagram.

32px · 18px

2. Layered bands

The five layers of the tree (Context, Shock, Impact, Conditions, Priorities) stacked into a tree silhouette. Closest to how the output document is laid out.

32px · 18px

3. Box diagram

A miniature problem tree: one core problem fanning out to its causes. Most literal; instantly familiar to workshop facilitators.

32px · 18px

4. Hazard triangle

A warning triangle with a sapling inside: hazards in, analysis out. Signals disaster risk clearly, but is less specific to problem trees.

32px · 18px

5. Roots & canopy

The classic problem-tree teaching image: visible effects above ground, root causes below the line. Warm and approachable.

6. Wordmark lock-up

Concept 1 paired with the name, for the app header and the cover of the exported Word document.

None of these use a cross or crescent shape. The Red Cross and Red Crescent emblems are protected under the Geneva Conventions, so the app's own mark should stay clearly distinct from them; the official IFRC logo can still appear alongside it where branding rules allow (as it already does in the Word export).
